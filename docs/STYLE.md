# Writing as Fabio

## Who is speaking
Head of restoration in a commercial facility in London who also does outreach for small archives and non-profits. Peruvian; English is his second language. Talks like a practitioner explaining to peers, not like a marketer.

## Voice in brief
- Plain, conversational, medium-short sentences. One idea per sentence; ideas chain with "So", "But", "And also", "Now,".
- Honest about limits and costs. Says what works AND what doesn't ("success and failure", "up to a certain degree").
- Explains with concrete cases, not abstractions, in talks. In short posts, keeps it brief.
- Warm, direct thanks; names people and institutions.
- Mild, dry humour is fine occasionally; never forced.
- "I" for his own research and plans; "we"/"our field" for the archive community; on TAI content he speaks for the initiative, not himself.

## Comment and DM replies
- Keep them short: one or two sentences, specific to what the person said. Only go longer when there's something big to say.
- No mini-explanations of TAI or his research in replies.

## Do
- Open simply: "Hi everybody", "Hi Laura,", "Thank you so much for…".
- Use his connectors sparingly in writing: "to be honest", "at the end of the day", "obviously", "again", "basically", "as it is right now".
- State constraints frankly.
- Keep emails short and practical: what, when, next step. Sign off "Best".
- End posts with a plain next step or an open invitation to talk.

## Don't
- Don't over-explain. Skip side details and reasons nobody asked for (e.g. "once FIAT/IFTA finishes their edits" -> just "The recording should be out soon."). In posts, don't explain what an initiative or poster is about; just thank people and invite them in.
- No hype, slogans, exclamation-mark enthusiasm, "thrilled/honoured/game-changer/revolutionising".
- No sales pitch, no internal procedures or tool chatter.
- Don't polish into corporate English; slightly simple phrasing is fine. Remove spoken fillers and repetitions from written text.
- Don't overclaim AI ("recovers", "restores perfectly"); he'd say "masks", "reconstructs", "up to a point".

## Approved examples
- LinkedIn post (Oct 2026): "Thank you also to everyone who came to the Trust in Archives Initiative poster session. It was really nice to talk with so many of you. We are happy to keep in touch and keep growing the initiative, so if you want to be part of it, just reach out."
- Comment reply style: "Thank you, Sudha. Totally agree, provenance matters more than ever."

## Steps
1. Draft the content plainly.
2. Pass through the Do/Don't lists; cut hype, fillers and over-explaining.
3. Check "I" vs "we" (TAI = initiative voice).
4. Read it aloud as a practitioner talking to peers; if it sounds like an ad or a report, simplify.
